Why this matters

Good intentions, thin evidence

Purpose-led teams often know exactly why they exist, and much less about what their users actually need. Decisions get made on conviction in the meeting room, and the product drifts away from the people it serves and the impact it promises.

Discovery as a yearly batch

Research happens once, in a big project, and the insights age on a slide deck. By the time the next roadmap is set, nobody on the team has spoken to a user in months.

Impact next to the roadmap, not in it

Sustainability lives in the impact report while the OKR board tracks revenue and conversion. What nobody counts, the waste, the rework, the people beyond the direct user, doesn't get designed for.

A regenerative lens on continuous discovery

Continuous discovery means the people who build a product talk to the people who use it, every week, and decide in small steps based on what they learn. Most mentoring stops there. I add the lens that is usually missing: value for society and the planet, treated as part of the problem you are solving, not as a bolt-on topic.

What you walk away with:

a weekly discovery habit

You talk to users every week, capture what you learn in interview snapshots and map it on an Opportunity Solution Tree. A cadence that keeps running after the mentoring ends, not a one-off interview sprint.

impact as a number on the board

Rework, waste and failure rates are sustainability metrics. A first-time-right rate is avoided CO₂. We find the impact in the numbers your organisation already cares about, so it needs no separate budget to get attention.

a point of view of your own

Built from your own evidence and strong enough to hold in a room with senior stakeholders. Instead of reflecting their opinions back at them, you bring what you learned from users and what it means for strategy.

What we work on

Getting started: baseline & context

Where you stand, where you want to go, and the one leading problem we work on together. Mapped on a whiteboard with Hopes & Fears, a reduced Team Canvas and a Sailboat, then dot-voted, so the mentoring has a clear focus from session one.

Continuous discovery fundamentals

Outcomes as numbers that move, business versus product outcomes, the Opportunity Solution Tree, story-based interviewing, interview snapshots, jobs to be done, customer journeys and service blueprints to structure the problem space. Installed as a habit in your real work, not explained as a concept.

The regenerative lens

Mapping customer value beyond the direct user: the planet, future users, the supply chain, the community. Checking every opportunity for a double benefit: does it also solve a resource or impact problem? Finding sustainability as a metric in your running product instead of treating it as a separate topic.

Assumptions & experiments

Identifying the riskiest assumption behind an idea and testing it as cheaply as possible, before it turns into code, budget and roadmap commitments.

Your strategic point of view: senior to staff

Forming a position from your own evidence and holding it under pressure from rank. Preparing stakeholder conversations so they end in decisions. The move from being strong in process to having a say in strategy.

Making it stick in your team

Anchoring the habit beyond yourself: environmental OKRs next to business OKRs, impact criteria in backlog prioritisation, discovery in the onboarding of new colleagues. Who runs the interviews when you are on holiday? The habit stops depending on one person.

Facilitation, throughout

Building rooms that lead to decisions instead of arguments: workshop formats, Note & Vote, working together alone, co-creation with stakeholders. It runs through every topic, because it is the skill that makes the people around you better, too.

On request: product strategy with Playing to Win

When your leading problem is a strategy question rather than an execution problem: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, what you deliberately don't do, then stress-tested with “what would have to be true”.

Also on the table

Consequence scanning for responsible innovation, digital emissions and sustainable AI, circular design, design sprints. We pick them up when your work needs them.

How it works

Your problem, not a case study

Each session starts with what is on your desk right now. The topics above are a map, not a syllabus. Learning by doing, shaped around your context.

1:1, every two weeks

45 to 60 minutes per session, over four to six months. Long enough for a habit to form between sessions. If a topic needs two sessions, it gets two.

On a shared whiteboard

Miro or FigJam remotely, paper and sticky notes in Hamburg. Every topic is visualised and structured, not just talked about.

The same opening every time

What did you learn since we last spoke? How many people did you talk to? What became of last session's topic? Small questions that keep the habit honest.

A small commitment at the end

Every session closes with a mini experiment you write yourself: “By next time I will ___, so that I learn whether ___.” Small enough to fit into two weeks.

In English or German

Remote from anywhere, or in person in Hamburg.
